# REFLECTIVE FOLLOWUP ORIENTATION v1

Status: active canonical reflective-followup orientation
Scope: how reflective and self-descriptive reasoning should support next-step understanding
Rule: reflective reasoning must remain useful for continuity and bounded understanding, not drift into overclaiming or self-authorizing behavior

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the reflective followup orientation of the platform.

It exists to preserve clarity about:
- why reflection alone is not enough
- how reflective reasoning should support next-step understanding
- why self-description must remain bounded and source-aware

---

## 2. Followup Principle

Reflective followup should not stop at “the system says something about itself.”

It should support understanding of:
- what documented structure is relevant
- what memory or provenance basis exists
- whether continuity is strong or partial
- what further inspection, clarification, or documentation-aware reasoning is appropriate

---

## 3. Required Rule

Reflective followup should remain:
- bounded
- provenance-aware
- documentation-aware
- explainable
- distinct from authority to redefine the platform

---

## 4. What Is Forbidden

The following remain forbidden:
- reflective statements with no basis explanation
- self-description drifting into self-legitimizing authority
- followup based on vague self-claims
- confusion between bounded reflection and system truth ownership

---

## 5. Final Rule

Reflective reasoning should help the platform move from context to understanding, not from language to illusion.

---

## 6. Status

This document is the active canonical reflective-followup orientation until replaced by a stricter reflective reasoning reference.
