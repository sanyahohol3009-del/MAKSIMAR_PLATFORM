# FALLBACK AND CONTINUITY ORIENTATION v1

Status: active canonical fallback-and-continuity orientation
Scope: how fallback behavior should preserve continuity in mobile/bridge/accelerator contexts
Rule: fallback must remain a continuity-preserving operational concept, not an undocumented downgrade into confusion

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the fallback and continuity orientation of the platform.

It exists to preserve clarity about:
- why fallback matters
- how fallback differs from failure
- why continuity must survive backend reduction or external detachment
- how users and operators should interpret non-ideal but legitimate operating modes

---

## 2. Fallback Principle

Fallback is not the same as collapse.

Fallback may preserve:
- reduced but still legitimate operation
- continuity of user-facing experience
- trust in system stability
- safety under degraded, detached, or constrained conditions

---

## 3. Required Rule

Fallback and continuity should remain explainable in terms of:
- what changed
- what capability was reduced
- why continuity is still preserved
- why the system remains legitimate even without the stronger mode

---

## 4. What Is Forbidden

The following remain forbidden:
- fallback treated as undocumented weirdness
- degraded or reduced mode framed as illegitimate existence
- continuity broken because the best mode disappeared
- operator or user confusion about whether the platform is still valid

---

## 5. Final Rule

A mature mobile extension layer preserves continuity across mode loss, not only performance in ideal conditions.

---

## 6. Status

This document is the active canonical fallback-and-continuity orientation until replaced by a stricter mobile fallback reference.
