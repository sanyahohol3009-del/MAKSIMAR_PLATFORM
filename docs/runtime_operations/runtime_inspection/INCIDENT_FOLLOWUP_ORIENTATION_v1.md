# INCIDENT FOLLOWUP ORIENTATION v1

Status: active canonical incident-followup orientation
Scope: operator-facing understanding of what follows a runtime incident
Rule: incident followup must remain an explicit operational concept rather than an improvised emotional reaction to failure

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the incident followup orientation of the platform.

It exists to preserve clarity about:
- what an incident means operationally
- why incident followup differs from ordinary runtime inspection
- why failed or degraded conditions should still preserve diagnostic continuity

---

## 2. Incident Followup Principle

Incident followup is not only “fix it fast.”

It should preserve:
- interpretation of what happened
- continuity of incident meaning
- bounded operator response
- distinction between diagnosis, recovery, and future prevention

---

## 3. Required Rule

The platform should remain able to support understanding of:
- whether an incident occurred
- what class of incident it appears to be
- what immediate state the runtime is left in
- whether recovery, inspection, or deeper analysis is the next legitimate step

---

## 4. What Is Forbidden

The following remain forbidden:
- treating every incident as identical
- losing diagnostic meaning during reaction
- restart as a substitute for thinking
- incident followup performed only by habit with no explicit model

---

## 5. Final Rule

Incident followup is part of runtime maturity, not merely cleanup after embarrassment.

---

## 6. Status

This document is the active canonical incident-followup orientation until replaced by a stricter incident-operations reference.
